# Bioregional writing plan

Started 2026-10-12. A checklist for turning the Cambridge chapter ("Bioregional Design: Rethinking Furniture Production Through Local Systems and Regeneration") into wiki tiddlers and short essays, one section at a time.

## How to use this

- One small item a day. One essay a week. Check the box when the tiddler exists, even if it is rough.
- Each week follows one section of the chapter, so the reading and the writing stay in the same place.
- Skip around freely. Nothing here depends on order except the items marked **needs Justin**.
- To get a head start on a week, tell Claude: "skeleton week 3". Claude writes the factual shell for the notes and chairs (maker, year, materials, source link, tags) and leaves the claim to you.
- To get a draft to react to, tell Claude: "draft [title]". Expect to rewrite it.

**The one rule:** write every piece fresh, from your notes and interviews. Don't paste sentences from the chapter. The ideas, facts, and interviews are yours to reuse. The chapter's wording is the publisher's.

**Kinds of item**

- `note` — a concept or reference tiddler, 50 to 150 words
- `chair` — a `Chair-ness` entry: who, when, what it is made of, and one line on what it argues. Tag by era and by cycle (`Biological Nutrient`, `Technical Nutrient`, `Monstrous Hybrid`)
- `essay` — 500 to 1,000 words, starting from the concrete case named

New work gets the tag `Bioregional Design`, to keep it apart from the `SDCSC` chapter drafts.

## Drafts waiting for your rewrite

### Material Shed

```
A watershed is all the land that drains to one river. A material shed is the same idea for stuff: the area a thing's materials come from, get worked in, and go back to.

The [[Oka Chair]] has a small one. The oak fell in Baltimore. The wool grew on a farm in Pennsylvania, and was washed and carded on that farm. The frame was made at [[Open Works]], also in Baltimore. When the chair is worn out it can rot in the region it grew in.

Most things have a material shed the size of the planet. Nobody could draw it.

Try drawing yours for one object on your desk.

I didn't invent the word. I'm choosing to use it this way. It follows watershed, foodshed, and [[Fibershed]], Rebecca Burgess's name for the region that could clothe you. A material shed widens that from fiber to everything a thing is made of.

See [[BioRegion]], [[Fibershed]], [[Waste = Food]]
```

### Scaling Out

```
The farm has 200 yards of wool. So the design uses 200 yards.

That is the whole method. [[Common Objects]] sizes the [[Oka Chair]] to what its suppliers can make without changing how they farm. When the wool runs out, the chairs stop.

A company that wants more chairs than that has two choices. It can push the farm to grow, or it can find a second farm somewhere else. The second choice is scaling out. The next Oka Chair is planned for Michigan, with alpaca in place of sheep's wool and whatever timber falls there. Same idea, different chair.

The permacomputing people got here first: [[Scalability downwards is more significant]].

Open question: what does the customer do while they wait?
```
