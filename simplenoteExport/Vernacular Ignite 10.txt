Contemporary designers upon hearing the word “vernacular” often conjure images of hand-painted signs and quaint candy wrappers. But, the vernacular is more than a collection of quotable styles and false nostalgia—it is way of thinking. Vernacular objects—from houses to butter churners—progressed through a cautious, evolving methodology (or in design parlance, a “system”) at the foundation of which are various constraints. The principles of the vernacular design methodology are the same as those at the heart of the open source movement and currently trendy “systems- / rules-based design” methodologies. We'll quickly look at and define some of the principles themselves and see a few ways in which they're used—historically and currently. Incorporating these principles in new and inventive ways can help guide our future designs towards the reliable, the iterable, and the sustainable.
