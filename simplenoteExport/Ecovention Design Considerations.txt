Ecovention Design Considerations.

Choices for how this book was designed were influenced by two of the common “3R's” — reuse and reduce. Sue and I previously worked on a book titles “Green Acres Artists Farming Fields, Greenhouses and Abandoned Lots.” The grid for Green Acres had been developed according to some important conceptual reasoning, and had a lot of time invested into it. Since Ecovention Europe can be seen as some what of a follow up to Green Acres, we had the idea to “reuse” the templates for that book. As such, the type choices, grid setup, etc. are all lifted from Green Acres and tweaked for use on this project. This is one way that I see reuse as an important part of a sustainable design practice — all resources and energy should be conserved — reuse of ideas and tools is just as useful in this endeavor as materials. In terms of reduction, we also hoped to use less material and ink in the manufacture of this book. One way I attempted this was in the creation for the section’s color palette. I determined that it was important to reduce ink coverage as much as possible, so no color is more that 100% coverage additively (In the CMYK model, colors all start at 100% pure C, M, Y, or K... and then are mixed in fractions to keep 100% or less total coverage... 50% + 50%; 33% + 33% + 33%, etc.). I also used bitmapped images as the color swatches to further reduce the amount of ink coverage (since there are so many pixels w/ 0% coverage, the feel of a filled area is still kept, but much less ink is still used), while still providing a color swatch. Every attempt to keep things from bleeding and to fill each page’s grid completely was also made.
