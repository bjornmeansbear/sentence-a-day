[GD3] Project 1: Reverse Storyboard

Time-based communication introduces new sets of conditions that change the nature of “sketching.” The method used to sketch a temporal idea is the storyboard. Storyboards are visual representations that aid in the the creation process of digital storytelling. Storyboards outline the narrative and develops compositional models for type, images, image frames, transitions, and sound. These are then laid out in sequential order to create the the flow of a production. They can also include technical aspects and explanations of design. 

For this project I need you to each pick a piece that you think counts as a “Motion Design” piece — it needs to be at least 30 seconds in length. 

Next, develop a well organized storyboard of the piece you choose using whatever method you so choose (however, you must be able to present us the storyboard on the projector). Use still images, drawings, photos, and text to model and articulate what is occurring in the piece. Selected images should represent definitive changes: a cut, transition, or some other obvious change occurring on screen. Your assessment may also need notes articulating the basics of movement, sound, image, symbol, camera view, typographic gesture, and pacing if these are not obvious from your stills.

Storyboarding is a way to lay out the flow and order of the text and its corresponding components. Think of a storyboard as a map of your presentation that allows you to arrange all of the visual materials in the order you want them before you insert them into presentation software.

You can sketch your plans on paper, use the table function in your word processor, or invest in storyboarding software—and make revisions as you go along. 

Whichever method you choose, be sure to ask yourself these questions:
    • Where do we need explanatory text or introductions?
    • Where do we need transition text or a segue to another topic?
    • Where do we want each image to appear on the page—in relation to which portion of text, or other components?
