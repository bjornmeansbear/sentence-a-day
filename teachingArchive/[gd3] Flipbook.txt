Flipbook
The final project will be making a flipbook. This is meant to be a culmination of everything we have discussed and examined so far this term. The exact meaning of what a “flipbook” is is up to you to interpret, and you do not have to make a physical book if your concept/idea works better purely digitally. However, you MUST adhere to the following guidelines:

1. If making a physical book, or using stop-motion, you must have at minimum 90 pages/frames. If you are doing something digital, and the piece has a concrete duration, it must last at least 3 seconds … For those that are doing something more interactive, or web or generative, these rules might not apply, so ask me for further clarification.

2. You must be able to explain how your piece qualifies as a "book"
    - if you are making a physical flipbook then you semi-automatically accomplish this…
    - if doing something on screen, you might need to explain what you think "book" means in this new space.

3. This needs to be a piece of graphic design still. How does time, motion, and graphic design translate into the space of a book? Analyze this in your design work.

4. I'd like you all to keep thinking about the different things we've been talking about all term — physics, time, the universe expanding, light speed, entropy, systems … etc. These are all things that I find important when working with Time and Motion in Design, but also just for design in general. However, I think the Maholy-Nagy + Futurist essays will bring up some interesting ideas about how traditional 2D mediums can still embody the ideas of time and motion without having to be films or interactive works.

Fall 2010
Graphic Design 3
Project 3: Flipbook (final Project)

-----

For our final project you will all be making a flipbook.
This is meant to be a culmination of everything we have discussed and examined so far this term. Now the exact meaning of what a “flipbook” is is up to you to interpret, and you do not have to make a physical book if your concept/idea works better purely digitally. However, you MUST adhere to one of these two guidelines:
    
1. Use of Stop Motion - the idea of the flipbook is to actually capture things frame by frame, so my preference is that you actually make each frame.

or

2. If you don’t use stop motion (IE use tweening to create your transitions) you MUST output your design-a-mation as a physical book. I repeat — if you do not make a stop motion design-a-mation, you MUST print out and make a physical flipbook of your work.

ALSO
the bare minimum is 50 frames … if you have less than this, and you actually construct a physical book, there is no way you’ll have enough paper to actually grip and flip. I would say shoot for higher than that, as it will give you more opportunity for narrative—50 frames is the BARE MINIMUM.

-----

Prompts:
Okay, this class is supposed to be about time and motion.
I want you to make a flipbook that covers uses these ideas/elements as prompts:

A. Time + Motion - what does this mean to you?
B. Narrative + Storytelling - part of the advantage to sequential art is that it can easily carry a narrative, I want you to come up with some sort of narrative device for your flipbooks.
C. Entropy - this is the idea that everything in the universe tends towards chaos, no matter how ordered you try to make it.

----

Requirements
1. Either Stop Motion or a Physical book or both
2. @ the minimum 50 frames
3. When making physical books you must make at least 2 flipbooks so that I can keep one if you make a physical version (an edition of 5 probably isn't a bad idea, being able to trade projects amongst each other is always valuable)
4. Discusses the ideas of Time + Motion, & Entropy
5. Must have some sort of “narrative”
6. Make sure the book / animation feels “Designed” …

The rest is up to you.

----

Helpful Hints / Contstruction Guidelines
—
Paper stock
- note cards work well, a little thinner than this would be fine too
—
Size/Shape
- usually better if slightly wider than tall, remember, you only see about 1/3 of the outer edge of the page in a real, physical flipbook when flipping …
—
conceptual:
- why does this need to be in motion
- how does “flipping” through aid the concept
—
needs “design” components … type?
- Remember to aim for “graphic design” over animation or illustration
—
Don’t forget that once you get to the end of the flipbook you can turn it over and flipback
- this could be an interesting conceptual element to the piece—this is also a way to use less physical pages, but still achieve the same minimum 70 pages/frames/slides
