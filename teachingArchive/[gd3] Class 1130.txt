Use these in our actual in class discussion.

Discussion Questions:

Space Time Problems
There is explanation of 1D, 2D, and 3D architectural ideas at the beginning of this piece. Have we moved onto a 4D ideal for how architecture or design works?

Space-Time: Does the way Maholy-Nagy discuss "Space-Time" mesh or conflict with other things we've talked about this term?

How do we grasp motion in space?
Or rather, what ways do we have of capturing motion in space?

What is "Illustionist Motion"
Do you ever use it in GD? Can you? Why would you?

"Cubist paintings render objects as if the spectator is moving around them in space"
Can you think of useful things today that do this same thing?
Were these simply avant-garde precursors to today's 3D models?

How does moving things at a high speed effect (affect?) their appearance?
Are there ways to design with this motion in mind?
Can you think of any good ones?
What about modern solutions that also take into account motion of the graphics being designed?

Are concerns like "Speed" still important ones?
Is this essay just completely dated?
Have we found other solutions? Do we have other problems?

Any ideas as to what "Vision-in-Motion" means?

Can you use Speed, Motion, ETc. statically for any emotional or aesthetic benefit?
What were the Cubists + Futurists using it for then?

Are Stroboscopic photos still Motion Graphics?

Are Photos + Video still the "best" way to "capture" motion?

Can you think of other examples of Frozen Movements besides what Maholy Nagy brings up in this essay? Other art or design movements? What about 80's/90's deconstructionist design?

Futurist Manifesto
Can you pull out what sorts of technologies + machines are mentioned here?
(Are we more or less tech/machine driven today?)

Who and What does Marianetti celebrate?

Can comparisons be made between their embracing of industry and our modern embracing of web and computer tehcnology?

Marianetti was an Agist, Masogynist, Racist, and Facist — should we care at all about his manifesto because/despite this?

This was a movement built on newness, velocity, sontaneity, violence, anarchy, and destruction … does it sound familiar with anything today?

The futurists made work that sort of was at the foundation of the modern GD practice. Is any of this valuable to contemporary GD today?
