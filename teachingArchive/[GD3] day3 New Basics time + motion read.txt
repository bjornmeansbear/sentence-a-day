[GD3] day3 New Basics time + motion reading notes

Any word or image that moves functions both spatially and temporally

time and motion are considerations for all design work, from a multipage printed book, whose pages follow each other in time, to animations for film and TV that have literal duration.

Animation encompasses diverse modes of visible change
 - there is literal movement
 - also changes in scale, transparency, color, later, and more (these are not unlike Aristotle's 4 kinds of changes... which he describes as "motion")
  
It can be useful to think about the screen as an active, changing surface as well as a neutral stage or support onto which characters are rushed on and off.

A word or design element can stay still while the environment around it changes

Motion designers must think both like painters and typographers and like animators and filmmakers.

The thing to keep in mind is the idea of change...
next is "interactivity"
    - how to the changing objects interact with each other?
    - how does one thing changing affect (effect?) the other things changing.
    
Showing images in a row is an accepted way to represent time or movement on a two-dimensional surface.

perisistence of vision:
the retina retains images for a split second longer than the images are actually before us, resulting in the illustion of movement when numerous images appear in rapid succession. 

frame-by-frame:
the series of still frames are manually (or digitially/automatically created). These are actual still frames, one drawing (photo, or board, or whatever) exists for each frame of video
    - this is what you're doing in stop motion obviously
    - you must manually create all the deviations in scale, color, movement, etc.

Keyframes:
Fixed states that a lead designer/animator draws or creates.

Normally first / last frames of each short sequence of action, indicating the start and conclusion of one or more important changes.

inbetweeners:
originally actual artists, they fill in the gaps between keyframes by drawing all the missing "in-between" frames
These are then called "tweens"
these can be generated automatically by digital animation software
    (this is how flash and AE work)
