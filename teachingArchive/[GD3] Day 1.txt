3. Storyboarding basics
The first thing we need to discuss are storyboards. 
Does everyone know what a storyboard is?
Now, I'm giving you the most rudimentary explanation of what they are, but more info is available on the class website under "project 1"
Semantically the name describes it pretty well.
It is a tool for helping to organize and outline time + motion pieces. Things with duration or with movement or both — It is basically sketching for time + motion
Think of it kind of like a comic strip.
All the important information is there, and you put the rest together.
It can show action, camera movements (like pan or zoom), etc.

Basics of a narrative arc?

Any word or image that moves functions both spatially and temporally

Time and motion are considerations for all design work, from a multipage printed book, whose pages follow each other in time, to animations for film and television, which have literal duration.

Animation encompasses diverse modes of visible change.

Showing images in a row is an accepted way to represent time or movement on a 2D surface. Drawings or photos become like words in a sentence, linked together to tell a story. 

All animation (and motion) consists of change over time.
