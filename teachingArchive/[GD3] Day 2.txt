PLATO + ARISTOTLE
- there was nothing that even barely resembled modern science in their day, philosophy was to them "science"
- to understand plato + aristotle we must understand their writings and thoughts as them trying to scientifically describe the real world … plato was more caught up with ethics and politics, aristotle really impacted science the most...

\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\

Plato's thoughts on motion:
- interested in organizing a critique of pure reason as the instrument for obtaining scientific knowledge
    * these are thought experiements, and logical arguments, not actual scientific study, which is why he and artistotle ended up being so wrong about so many physical realities.

- Every object has two constitutional aspects: 
    1. its matter
        * matter is the "stuff" that something is comprised of
    2. its form
        * form is the integral meaning of the object, the sort of -ness of the object or thing, the universal guidelines for somethings being

- Motion itself cannot be perceived, only bodies in motion 

- Hypothesis:
    plato is the first to really use this, basically a primordial judgement accepted as true — often called "prior knowledge" — though no logical proog can be adduced. 
    
From one idea a great company of cognate ideas may then be developed
    - using antitheticals mostly:
        1. same / different
        2. like / unlike
        3. motion / rest (physics)
        4. equal / unequal (math)
        
        
\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\\

Aristotle's Thoughts on Motion:
(Almost everyone respects his name, but almost no one reads what he wrote)

rejected plato's forms, though he just came up with his own version of the same idea (for example, oxygen can have oxygen-ness, but it can also bond with hydrogen, and then get "water-ness" instead [although one could then argue that part of "oxygen-ness" is to be able to bond with hydrogen and form water, or carbon to form carbon dioxide, but whatever])

    -> better example … Marble -> statue. Neither "marble-ness" nor "statue-ness" can explain how a person can carve the marble into the statue, the activity of the sculptor does this, and this isn't a "form" you can name.

To know the causes of a change, one must recognize the matter as a cause in the sense that it underlies the whole change and actually receives the new form...

physics to aristotle was the philosophy of nature
    - physics to him was the science of natures and of natural changes — astronomy, physics, chemisty, biology + pschology
    
Of things that exist, some exist by nature, and some exist by other causes.
Things that exist by nature has within itself a principle of motion and of stationariness
Things from other causes have no innate impulse to change [ though their materials might ]
Nature is a source or cause of being moved/being at rest
natural things have within them the controls for the process of their creation

Natural causes, and then there are chance and spontaneity
Nature belongs to the class of causes which act for the sake of something.

The Four Causes:
1. Material Cause: that out of which something is made
2. Efficient Cause: that by which something is made
3. Formal Cause: that into which something is made
4. Final Cause: that for the sake of which something is made.
