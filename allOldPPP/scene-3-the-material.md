# People Processing Plastic
## Scene III — The Material

**THE GUEST:** Can I ask — why plastic? Why not just... stop using it?

**DEV:** We'd love that. For new plastic, yes. Stop. Completely.

**MARA:** But there's a problem with that position if you say it without the second part.

**THE GUEST:** What's the second part?

**MARA:** All the plastic we could ever need already exists. It's out there right now. In the ocean. In the ground. Floating in various forms. We don't need to make a single new piece. We just need to stop pretending the old stuff has no value.

**DEV:** *(picking up a handful of shredded plastic flakes — multicolored, almost beautiful)* You know what this was?

**THE GUEST:** Bottles?

**DEV:** Bottle caps. Different colors because different brands. Same material. HDPE. High-density polyethylene.

**THE GUEST:** And what does it become?

**DEV:** Anything we want it to be.

*THE BOTTLE speaks again, warmer now.*

**THE BOTTLE:** This is what I've been trying to tell you.

**THE BOTTLE:** I am not a bottle. I was never really a bottle. I was a material that agreed, temporarily, to be a bottle. The shape was a loan. The substance is mine to keep.

**THE BOTTLE:** They made another me today. While I was still here.

**THE BOTTLE:** That is the part I find most bewildering.

**THE GUEST:** I've never thought about it that way.

**MARA:** Most people haven't. Because the system is designed so you don't have to. Designed so the new one is slightly cheaper, slightly easier to grab, and the question of what happened to the last one is someone else's job, somewhere else, later.

**DEV:** Linear thinking. Take, make, waste. In that order, every time, forever.

**THE GUEST:** As opposed to.

**MARA:** Circle. You already know what a circle is. You just haven't been asked to live inside one.

*THE MICROPLASTIC speaks again. Still patient. But there is something new in it now — not urgency exactly, but proximity. As if it has moved closer.*

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I have always been willing.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I am a polymer chain. I can link with others. I can become structure, density, surface, form. I contain the memory of oil and heat and pressure and time.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I did not choose to be small.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I was made large and then abandoned into smallness.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** There is a difference.

**DEV:** *(to THE GUEST, gently)* That's the thing about microplastics that most people miss. They're not a new substance. They're just plastic that got lost. Same material. Same potential. Just — unaccounted for. Circulating in a system that built no way to bring them back.

**THE GUEST:** Can you get them back?

**MARA:** Some. Not all. Not yet. Which is exactly why we should stop making new ones and start building the systems to recover what's already out there.
