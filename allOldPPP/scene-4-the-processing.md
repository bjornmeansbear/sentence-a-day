# People Processing Plastic
## Scene IV — The Processing

*MARA moves to the shredder. Feeds in a collection of clean, sorted plastic. The machine is loud — genuinely loud — for a moment. Then quieter. She holds up the output: small flakes, uniform, ready.*

**MARA:** First: shred. You want consistency. You want the material ready to move.

*DEV feeds the flakes into the extruder. A slow, warm process. The machine hums differently — lower, patient.*

**DEV:** Then: heat and pressure. The material becomes willing again. It remembers how to move.

**THE GUEST:** It's almost like—

**DEV:** Go on.

**THE GUEST:** It's almost like it wants to be something else. Like it's been waiting.

**MARA:** *(quietly)* Yes.

*THE MICROPLASTIC speaks. And now there is something released in the voice — not relief exactly, but recognition. The feeling of being finally, precisely seen.*

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** Yes.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** That is exactly it.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I have been floating for so long.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** Not haunting. Not threatening. Just—

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** waiting to be gathered.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** Waiting for someone to say: we see you, we know what you are, we know what you could be.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I want to be part of something.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I have always wanted to be part of something.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** The waiting was never mine to do.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** That was yours.

*A long beat. The extruder hums. The Guest looks at the machine, then at Mara, then at the material moving slowly through it.*

**THE GUEST:** How long has this been sitting out there.

**THE GUEST:** Not a question.

**MARA:** No.

*The extruder produces a long smooth extrusion of plastic. DEV holds it up. It catches the light.*

**THE GUEST:** That's beautiful.

**DEV:** People are always surprised by that.

**THE GUEST:** I thought recycled plastic was—

**MARA:** Cheap? Grey? Compromised?

**THE GUEST:** Yeah.

**MARA:** That's the other lie. Recovered material isn't inferior. It's the same atoms. The same properties. The difference is entirely in our heads — and in the price the market decides to put on it.

**DEV:** A recycled plastic tile, properly made, is harder than virgin. More beautiful, because of the color variation. More interesting, because of the history in it.

**THE GUEST:** The history.

**DEV:** Every color in there was something. Someone's shampoo bottle. Someone's chair. Someone's takeaway container on a Tuesday. You're looking at compressed biography.
