# ❌ ERRORS — fix these

## 1. "Two hundred degrees warm" — WRONG

**Line 413.** `CHRIS: Two hundred degrees warm. Don't.`

In an American mouth this reads as **200°F = 93°C.** HDPE doesn't melt until **120–130°C**, and extrusion runs at **180–240°C (356–464°F)**.

At 200°F the extruder would be producing warm solid flake, not a rope of plastic.

**Fix:** `Four hundred degrees warm. Don't.`
400°F ≈ 204°C — dead centre of the HDPE extrusion window, and it sounds more alarming, which is the point of the line.

> Sources: HDPE melting point 120–130°C; extrusion/injection 180–240°C, thermal degradation above 300°C. Laird Plastics HDPE Guide (2025); ICD Vietnam technical analysis.

# ⚠️ WATCH — defensible, but know the caveat

## 3. Ketchup caps and resin codes — a latent trap

Not an error as written, but the audience will connect two things you haven't linked:

- The Guest's tile is **"a two. HDPE."**
- The ketchup crate is red caps, no code stated
- Chris offers **"washed HDPE flake"** for the floor

**Ketchup bottle caps are polypropylene (PP, #5), not HDPE.** PP dominates flip-top and living-hinge closures — it survives thousands of bends. HDPE is milk jugs and detergent caps.

So the red tile pressed in Scene III is **PP #5**, and the floor Chris is pricing is **HDPE #2**. Different bins, different material.

**Three options:**

**A.** Leave it. Nothing on stage is false; the audience won't check.

**B.** One clarifying line — and it *strengthens* Kris's sorting argument:
> **CHRIS:** *(holding up a red cap)* Forty years of one family's ketchup.
> **KRIS:** That's a five, not a two. Different bin.
> **CHRIS:** I know it's a five.

**C.** Change the crate to detergent or milk caps (genuinely HDPE) and lose "ketchup."

B is the strongest — the disagreement is already their dynamic, and it demonstrates the precision the play is arguing for.

> Sources: PP (#5) and HDPE (#2) are ~90% of bottle caps globally. PP for carbonated drinks, screw caps, living hinges. HDPE for milk jugs, detergents. Pelliconi; IML Supplier; Propacks (2026).

# ✅ VERIFIED — these are solid

**Resin codes.** PET = 1 ✅ · HDPE = 2 ✅ · PVC = 3 ✅ · PP = 5 ✅ · ASTM D7611 ✅ · An RIC identifies polymer, not recyclability ✅

**"Polyester is PET. Resin code one. The same polymer as a water bottle."** ✅ Exactly right, and it's the fact most people don't know.

**"One load of polyester releases half a million fibers."** ✅ Napper & Thompson (2016), *Marine Pollution Bulletin* 112(1–2), 39–45. A 6 kg load of polyester released ~496,030 fibers. *(Acrylic hit ~729,000; polyester-cotton ~138,000.)* Measured into wastewater, not directly ocean — your line says "releases," which is correct.

**"Polyester is fifty-nine percent of all fiber made on earth."** ✅ Textile Exchange, *Materials Market Report 2025* (2024 data): polyester 59%, 78 million tonnes. All synthetics 69%. Cotton 19%.

**"I am a plastic fragment under five millimeters."** ✅ Standard definition.

**Microplastics in blood / lungs / breast milk.** ✅ All peer-reviewed:
- Blood — Leslie et al. (2022), *Environment International*, doi:10.1016/j.envint.2022.107199
- Lungs — Jenner et al. (2022), *Sci. Total Environ.*, doi:10.1016/j.scitotenv.2022.154907
- Breast milk — Ragusa et al. (2022), *Polymers* 14(13):2700
- Placenta — Ragusa et al. (2021), *Environment International*, doi:10.1016/j.envint.2020.106274

**"Particles under ten micrometers cross the placental barrier."** ✅ Supported. *"I was in your body before your body was born"* is a poetic gloss on real in-utero exposure evidence. Defensible.

**LVP is PVC, resin code 3, brand new plastic, almost none recycled.** ✅ All four correct. LVP core is PVC; multi-layer construction (wear layer, print film, core, backing) makes recovery impractical; most facilities can't process it.

**"Four dollars a square foot. Three hundred bucks."** ✅ LVP runs $2–5/sq ft materials. 70 sq ft × $4 = $280. Correct.

**"It isn't harder. It isn't stronger."** ✅ Correct, and good that Kris overrules Chris here. Reprocessing shortens polymer chains; recyclate is equal or slightly inferior, worse if polymers are mixed. Do not let anyone put "stronger than virgin" back in.

**Caps fall through screens at the MRF.** ✅ Standard sorting loss for small-format items. *(Note: newer facilities handle caps-on-bottles better, which is why "leave the cap on" is now the advice — your cap-on-bottle digression at line 263 is exactly right.)*

**Glass shatters in single stream and contaminates everything.** ✅ Well documented. *(The "300% maintenance costs" figure was softened to "sharply" — correct call, I could not source it.)*

**"One bin is cheaper to collect than three."** ✅ Single stream was adopted for collection economics — fewer trucks, less labor, higher participation. Recovery quality was the trade.

**"About three hundred pounds of plastic a year. Per person."** ✅ Greenpeace USA (2022): 51 million tons discarded in 2021 ÷ ~332M people = **307 lb**. Math checks.
