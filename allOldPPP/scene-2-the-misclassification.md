# People Processing Plastic
## Scene II — The Misclassification

**THE GUEST:** What do you do with it?

**MARA:** What do you think we do with it?

**THE GUEST:** Recycle it?

**MARA:** That word also does a lot of damage.

**THE GUEST:** How so?

**DEV:** Recycling, the way most people mean it — put it in the blue bin, feel better, don't think about it again — that's just outsourcing the guilt. Half of it goes to landfill anyway. More than half, depending on the year and the country and the mood of the market.

**THE GUEST:** So it doesn't work.

**MARA:** The bin system doesn't work. The material works fine.

*THE MICROPLASTIC speaks. Not from the walls, not from the air — from somewhere specific and small. A shaft of light, perhaps. A single point in the room. The voice is not frightening. It is patient. It has been waiting a long time to be heard.*

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I want to be a table.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I want to be a chair someone sits in for thirty years. A wall panel. A beam. A tile under someone's feet every morning.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I am small enough to be anything.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I have been in the ocean. In ice. In a fish, then in the person who ate the fish. Not because I wanted to be there. Because no one built a door for me to come back through.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I am not haunting you.

**THE MICROPLASTIC:** I am asking.

*A beat. The machines hum.*

**THE GUEST:** *(quietly)* What is that?

**MARA:** The part of the story no one tells properly.

**DEV:** Microplastics aren't a monster. They're a material that fell through every crack we built into the system. Polymer chains, wandering. Looking for somewhere to belong.

**THE GUEST:** That's — a generous way to think about it.

**MARA:** It's an accurate way. The problem was never the plastic. The problem was always the design of the system around it.

**THE GUEST:** So what's different about here?

**MARA:** Here we know what we have. People bring us specific things. HDPE. PET. Polypropylene. They separate it at home, in their office, at the café. It arrives clean. It arrives sorted. We don't have to undo anyone's mess.

**DEV:** The big system collects everything together and tries to sort it out later. Which is expensive and mostly doesn't work. We collect it already sorted. Because the people doing the collecting — they know what they have.

**THE GUEST:** That sounds... simple.

**MARA:** Most true things are.
