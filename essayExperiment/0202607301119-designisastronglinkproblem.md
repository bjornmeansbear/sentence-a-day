## Thoughts so far (not settled, just holding these)

- Maybe design isn't uniformly strong-link. Maybe different *phases* of the process have opposite dynamics, and that split is the actually teachable thing.
- Ethnographic research feels weak-link: one badly-run interview, one leading question, one moment of not really listening, and you can poison the whole team's understanding — even if the other five interviews were great. Garbage in, and everything built on top inherits the garbage.
- Prototyping / ideation feels strong-link, same as Mastroianni's science argument: generate twenty sketches, nineteen can be bad, costs nothing, because nobody builds on the bad ones. Only the one good idea propagates forward.
- If that split holds: self-consciousness in design usually comes from treating the *whole* process as weak-link — being precious/defensive about every sketch, as if a bad one damages the outcome. But if only research is actually weak-link, most of what design students get anxious about (an ugly first prototype) is anxiety spent in the wrong place.
- That might be the mechanism behind wanting the class to be better, faster, *and* less self-conscious, from one idea: loosen up where it's strong-link, tighten up where it's weak-link.
- Possible structure this suggests: a research lecture (protect against your worst input) + a prototyping lecture (chase your best output) + maybe a third on learning to tell which mode you're actually in.
- Reference point already circulating in design-thinking circles: the *Art & Fear* ceramics-class story — a class graded purely on quantity of pots ends up making better pots than the class trying to make one perfect pot. Somewhat a cliché at this point — decide whether to lean on it or deliberately avoid it since students may already know it.
- Still need: the actual concrete moment (a student prototype, a critique, something from my own practice) that had me thinking about this in the first place. That's what tells us if the two-phase framing holds up against something real.
