### Wk1 — updating the design-thinking critique
You've got Natasha Jen. The channel also has two things that would sharpen/update that opening:
- **Jenny Wen** (Design Lead at Anthropic, ex-Figma Director of Design) — "Why Designers Can No Longer Trust the Design Process" (Hatch Conference talk). Argues the standard DT sequence (research → personas → journey maps → problem statement → solutions) doesn't match how design actually happens now. A live, 2026-dated counterpoint to Jen's talk rather than just repeating "design thinking is bullshit."
- **Carly Ayres**, "Designers! Designers! Designers!" — several quotes saved on what AI does and doesn't replace in design work: research, problem framing, translating complexity into trusted experience; "Super ICs" who own outcomes end-to-end. Useful for making the case to students *why* HCD process skills matter more, not less, right now.

### Framing "research" itself (Wk1–2)
- **Celine Nguyen**, "research as leisure activity" — six saved quotes defining what research actually is: commitment to evidence, situating yourself in a discipline's history, culminating in an output, having both contemporary relevance and room for "useless" basic inquiry, requiring a community. Good framing text before LLAT — makes the case for *why* Learn/Look/Ask/Try isn't just busywork.
- **Adam Mastroianni**, "Science is a strong-link vs. weak-link problem" — heavily quoted (10+ saved blocks). Not in your current plan.

  **The point: design is a strong-link problem too.** Mastroianni's examples — venture capital (fine to fund a bunch of startups that fail if one goes huge), the Olympics (only your best athletes matter), music (bad songs don't actively hurt you, they're just ignored) — are structurally the same shape as a good brainstorm or prototyping sprint. You don't need every HMW or every low-fi prototype to be good; you need *one* of them to be good, and the weak ones cost you almost nothing.

  This directly backs up two things already in your plan without a named justification:
  - **Wk6 Ideate** — "everyone brainstorms off all the HMWs... put aside the majority of the less-good ideas." Mastroianni gives you the argument for *why* generating a big pile of mediocre ideas is the correct strategy, not a failure to be efficient.
  - **Wk9** — "Multiple low-fidelity prototypes... are more valuable than a single high-fidelity prototype." Same logic: quantity of attempts beats protecting any one attempt from being bad.

  Possible use: a short mini-lecture at the start of Wk6 (right before Ideate) — reframes "why are we making so many bad ideas on purpose" as a design principle, not a consolation prize. Worth naming the contrast case too: crit / final deliverable quality is arguably a *weak-link* problem — one broken piece of the final presentation (can't explain your process, no evidence) drags down the whole thing the way a bad transmission ruins a good engine. So: divergent phases (ideation, prototyping) = strong-link, generate freely; convergent phases (the final pitch) = weak-link, everything has to hold up.

  It's also the underlying justification for **bias-toward-action** (already one of your CLOs) and the **sacrificial prototype** concept in Wk4: if design is strong-link, a bad prototype costs you almost nothing, so the move is to make and prototype fast and frequently — fail in the open, in front of the group, and let that failure teach you and move the work forward — rather than protecting any single attempt from criticism. Worth stating explicitly to students: the failures aren't offset against your grade, they're the mechanism.
