# DI 200 — Prototyping Resource List

Links and practical advice for different modes of rapid prototyping, organized by fidelity/medium. For handing out at the Wk7 low-fidelity demo and referencing again at Wk9–13 as fidelity increases.

## Paper & Cardboard (Wk4, Wk7 — lowest fidelity, cheapest to fail)

The default starting material for a reason: near-zero cost, near-zero embodied energy, genuinely recyclable/compostable at end of life (see the Sustainability & Circularity throughline already in the syllabus — this isn't just the cheap option, it's the low-impact one).

- **"11 Ways to Cut and Connect Cardboard"** (Instructables) — already in your Are.na "aesthetics of prototypes" collection; a real technique reference for joints, folds, and connections beyond just taping box flaps together. Good as a pre-Wk7 handout.
- **"How to Make a Cardboard Prototype"** (video, also in that Are.na channel) — screen this at the start of the Wk7 demo rather than describing the technique verbally.
- **Carolyn Li-Madeo's balsa-wood/paper iPhone mockup** (arena.computer, in the same channel) — the reference image for "here's what fast, low fidelity looks like when done well."
- **Chairigami's cardboard sofa** and **Luo Studio's cardboard exhibition space** (same channel) — counter-examples showing cardboard taken to a genuinely high finish level. Useful for the Wk9 "increasing fidelity doesn't mean leaving the material behind" point.

**Advice:** hot glue and a cutting mat/blade beat tape and scissors for anything meant to survive a desk crit. Corrugated cardboard's grain direction matters for folds — fold with the flutes, not across them, or the fold cracks.

## Digital UI/Interaction Mockups (any week involving a screen-based service)

- **Figma** (figma.com) — the industry standard; free tier is sufficient for student work. Fastest path to a clickable prototype if the intervention is app/screen-based.
- **Penpot** (penpot.app) — free, open-source, self-hostable Figma alternative. Functionally comparable for this course's needs. Worth introducing explicitly as the F/LOS option, not just a footnote — consistent with the open-source-design thread already running through your own work (see `202007271900 The Libre Designer` and the F/LOS Daily material in the wiki).
- **Balsamiq** — deliberately low-fidelity, sketchy-looking wireframes. Useful if a team's early digital mockups are looking too polished too soon and you want to force them back down in fidelity.

**Advice:** for Wk7-level work, resist Figma/Penpot's temptation toward polish. A wireframe with boxes and arrows is more honest sacrificial-prototype material than a high-fidelity mockup made too early — save the pixel-perfect pass for Wk11–13.

## 3D & Physical Object Prototyping (Wk9–13, increasing fidelity)

- **Foam core** — the natural next step up from cardboard; cleaner cuts, still cheap, still fast.
- **3D printing** — check MICA's shop/fab-lab access and turnaround times before assigning; printing queues can blow past a one-week iteration cycle if not planned for.
- **Balsa wood** — good for anything needing a bit more structural rigidity than foam core without jumping to a shop tool.

**Advice:** the standing Wk9–13 question ("can you scale to actual size? cardboard to plywood? 3D model to 3D print?") is really asking whether a team has a reason to move up in fidelity, not just access to fancier materials. Don't let material upgrades substitute for actually answering an open question from the last iteration.

## Service & Interaction Prototyping (for interventions that aren't objects)

- **Storyboarding** — already in the syllabus's toolkit list; the fastest way to prototype a multi-step service or interaction before building anything physical.
- **Wizard-of-Oz prototyping** — fake the "smart" part of a system with a hidden human performing it. Directly useful for anything staff- or tech-mediated at the Hilton (a proposed check-in flow, a signage system) — you don't need working tech to test whether the *interaction* works.
- **Bodystorming** — physically walk through a proposed service flow with students playing "guest" and "staff" roles. Good for the arrival/front-desk and back-of-house briefs specifically, since those are fundamentally about people moving through a sequence, not an object.

**Advice:** for any Hilton brief that's really about a *service* (arrival, wayfinding, back-of-house workflow) rather than an *object*, push teams toward storyboarding or bodystorming before they default to building a physical thing — the intervention might not need an artifact at all, and prototyping the interaction first reveals that faster than building something nobody asked for.

## Choosing a Mode

Quick heuristic for teams unsure where to start: if the intervention could be described as "a person does X, then Y happens," prototype it as a storyboard or bodystorm first. If it's "a guest touches/holds/looks at an object," go straight to cardboard. If it's "a guest uses a screen," go straight to Balsamiq or a paper wireframe before touching Figma/Penpot. In all three cases, the sacrificial-prototype rule still applies: whichever mode you pick, the first pass should be fast and ugly on purpose.
